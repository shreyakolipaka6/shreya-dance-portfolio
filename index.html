About

The Art Behind
the Physician

My Story I began my journey in classical Bharatanatyam at age five, learning to tell stories through intentional movement, expression, and rhythm. Years of dedicated training culminated in my Arengetram in 2019 — a two-hour solo recital marking my graduation from student to teacher. From that first performance to stages across the country, dance has been the constant thread of my life.

The Practice Bharatanatyam demands the same precision medicine does — attention to minute details, composure under a spotlight, and the ability to react and adjust in real time with little margin for error. In college I expanded into contemporary and hip-hop, and as Co-Captain of Delaware Kamaal, a Bollywood-fusion team at the University of Delaware, I choreographed routines, led practices, and guided a team of dancers through performances — including a full transition to Zoom during COVID-19.

Dance & Medicine Medicine is an art form too — it demands quick thinking under pressure, skillful communication, and unwavering commitment. Leading performances with up to 40 people, not all of them dancers, taught me patience, adaptability, and how to bring out the best in others. These are the same qualities I bring to patient care. Every time I step onto a stage or into a clinic, I am fully present — and that is something dance taught me first.
